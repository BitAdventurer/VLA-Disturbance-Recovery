\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action policies execute predicted action chunks open loop, so after an object is suddenly displaced, the robot follows a plan made for the old scene. On LIBERO-10, teleporting the target object by 4 to 6 cm, mostly as the gripper approaches, cuts the {\boldmath$\pi_{0.5}$} policy's success from 95 to 65 percent. Under one paired protocol, simple inference-time levers (replan frequency, integration steps, chunk selection) recover little. More computation at the next scheduled query recovers nothing measurable, and moving the query to the event with a learned camera-frame detector recovers zero to three points without adding policy queries per episode. Correcting the actions recovers more. A fixed-rule shift toward the estimated displacement gains about six points. An event-gated residual, cloned from a scripted shift on privileged state, refined with reinforcement learning as lightweight post-training and distilled into a vision-only student, raises success from 65.4 to 73.7 percent in a pre-registered test on 500 fresh initial states (gain 8.4 points, 95 percent interval 5.6 to 11.2; 2.9 points over the fixed rule), with no measurable change in undisturbed success.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have become a standard recipe for
language-conditioned manipulation~\cite{kim2024openvla,black2024pi0,pi05}.
Most now predict \emph{action chunks}~\cite{zhao2023act,chi2025diffusion}:
one query of the model yields $H$ actions, of which the robot
executes only the first $h\le H$ and discards the rest before the model is queried again. Chunking smooths motion and amortizes the backbone's cost but makes the
controller open loop between queries. If the scene changes
inside a chunk, the robot executes actions planned for the old scene
until the chunk is spent, and the next query sees a configuration the training data
rarely contains.

\input{fig_overview_res}

How large is this cost, and what recovers it? We study the question with the
official $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint for LIBERO~\cite{pi05,liu2023libero} under
a controlled disturbance: when the open gripper first comes within 12\,cm of
the object it is approaching (or at a random step if it has not by then), the object is teleported 4--6\,cm horizontally,
which cuts success on LIBERO-10 from 95.4\% to 64.7\%. Tracking the object
(Section~\ref{sec:diag}) shows that the policy often first closes the gripper nearer the \emph{old} position.

Recent work offers several responses. Adaptive-inference methods decide
when to replan, how much to compute per query, or which of several sampled
chunks to keep~\cite{liang2026aac,pan2026corrector,chun2025dasip,liu2025bid}; runtime
monitors detect execution failures~\cite{agia2024sentinel,xu2025faildetect,gu2025safe};
and residual reinforcement learning (RL) learns corrections on top of a fixed policy~\cite{yuan2025decorator,xiao2025pld}.
They are evaluated on different tasks and disturbances; a concurrent benchmark of mid-execution changes such as target relocation finds, for four of its 14 policies including $\pi_{0.5}$, that varying the query cadence does not close the gap, but tests no event-triggered replanning or action correction~\cite{zhang2026liberomax}. We test simple versions of each response under one protocol. Moving the query to the event helps by only a few points, and the new chunk seems to aim only partly at the new position; correcting the actions recovers more: about six of the 31 lost points with a fixed rule and eight to eleven with a detector-gated residual policy cloned and refined with RL as post-training around the VLA (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a paired, seeded protocol on LIBERO-10 in which $\pi_{0.5}$ often first closes the gripper nearer the old
        position of a target object displaced mid-execution: every method is compared with the mean of seven noise
        draws of the base policy
        (complementing studies that perturb the setup~\cite{fei2025liberoplus}
        or the robot's actions~\cite{guo2026robustvla,gao2026ogpo});
  \item evidence that when to replan matters less than how much to correct: inference levers recover little,
        more computation at the next scheduled query nothing measurable and replanning at the detected event
        at most a few points, whereas a fixed-rule shift gains about six;
  \item an event-gated residual recovery policy that adds 2.9 points over a fixed-rule shift in a pre-registered
        test on fresh initial states, still gains with displacements twice as large and causes no measurable change in
        undisturbed success: as post-training around the VLA, a privileged teacher
        trained by behavior cloning (BC) of a scripted shift followed by proximal policy optimization (PPO) is distilled into
        a vision-only student gated by a learned per-step detector that recognizes 87--90\% of displacements on held-out
        initial states within three control steps.\footnote{Code, trained modules and per-episode results:\newline \url{https://github.com/BitAdventurer/VLA-Disturbance-Recovery}}
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Adaptive chunk execution.}
AAC sets the executed prefix of a chunk from the entropy of sampled
chunks~\cite{liang2026aac}, bidirectional decoding selects among chunks
resampled at every step~\cite{liu2025bid}, RTC inpaints the next chunk
while the current one executes~\cite{black2025rtc}, DVAC executes the
low-variance prefix of a chunk and replans before its high-variance
actions~\cite{feng2026dvac}, and DA-SIP chooses the number of integration
steps per query with a difficulty classifier~\cite{chun2025dasip}. They balance reactivity, consistency and compute but detect no events. VLA-Corrector~\cite{pan2026corrector} does: it truncates the chunk when visual latents drift from a learned prediction and replans with gradient guidance, but adds no correction policy and tests displacement only on hardware.

\noindent\textbf{Runtime monitoring.}
Sentinel~\cite{agia2024sentinel}, FAIL-Detect~\cite{xu2025faildetect} and SAFE~\cite{gu2025safe} flag failures or halt the policy based on action consistency and task progress, on sequential out-of-distribution tests and on VLA features, respectively. Like VLA-Corrector, we use detection to \emph{act}, here on one external event.

\noindent\textbf{Correcting a fixed policy's actions.}
Residual RL~\cite{johannink2019residual} refines imitation-learned policies~\cite{yuan2025decorator,ankile2024resip} and a
frozen $\pi_0$, with the corrections then distilled into the VLA~\cite{xiao2025pld}, and latent-noise RL steers $\pi_0$ without changing its weights~\cite{wagenmaker2025dsrl}. Other work post-trains the VLA itself with online
RL~\cite{chen2025conrft,guo2025irevla}, which has been shown to help when an
object is moved mid-episode~\cite{liu2025rl4vla}, or with offline RL to recover from errors~\cite{gao2026ogpo}. A training-free operator offsets and
compresses a chunk toward perceived motion~\cite{zhang2026pacepath}, the
closest analogue of our fixed-rule shift; its authors name teleports as its weak case. TRANSIC gates a residual with a classifier learned from human interventions~\cite{jiang2024transic}; ours is zero except after a detected scene displacement,
distilled from a privileged teacher into a vision-only student as
in~\cite{ankile2024resip}, and compared with a fixed-rule shift.

\section{Setting and Diagnosis}\label{sec:diag}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Chunked Execution and the Disturbance}
A flow-based VLA maps an observation $o_t$ and instruction $\ell$ to a
chunk $\mathbf{A}_t=(a_t,\dots,a_{t+H-1})$ by conditional generation~\cite{lipman2022flow,oh2026ptdiff}, integrating a learned velocity
field for $n$ Euler steps~\cite{black2024pi0}; the robot
executes $h\le H$ actions and queries again. We use the released $\pi_{0.5}$ LIBERO checkpoint of openpi~\cite{pi05} with
$H=10$ and, by default, $(n,h)=(2,10)$ (95.4\% undisturbed in one draw, 95.1\% over three); openpi's example setting $(10,5)$ reaches 91.9\% (three draws) at $2.8\times$ the policy-query time and loses as much as $(2,10)$ under the displacement below (to 61.2\%, two draws). \emph{Policy-query time} sums, over an episode's queries, the round-trip latency of each query's setting, measured offline on one RTX 5090 (32.6\,ms at $n=2$, 44.1\,ms at $n=10$); it is an accounting figure, as simulation is synchronous.
Episodes follow the released script (520
control steps at 20\,Hz after 10 settling steps) on the 50 fixed initial
states of each of the ten tasks.

The \emph{object displacement} is applied once per episode: when the open gripper first comes within 12\,cm (end effector to object center)
of a movable object of interest (an object named in the task goal) that it
does not hold, that object is teleported horizontally by a distance drawn from
$[4,6]$\,cm in a uniformly random direction and its velocity is zeroed. If no approach has occurred by a random step in 50--170 (about 26\% of episodes, mostly in two tasks), the nearest free object of interest is displaced at that step. Direction and magnitude are seeded per task and initial state, so every method sees the same vectors. Each method run other than the static schedules also reuses the sampling-noise seed of one base draw and matches it in event step and displaced object in every episode, although a few pre-event trajectories differ; across base draws and static schedules the event step differs in 27\% and 46--56\% of episodes, respectively, mostly by one step. Success drops to 64.7\% (mean of seven noise draws); every
failure is a timeout.

\subsection{Why the Policy Fails}
We tracked the displaced object in 100 disturbed episodes (initial states 0--9 of each task, one base draw; Table~\ref{tab:diag}). All successes eventually lift it, but 46\% of
failures never do. In about 62\% of \emph{both} groups the first gripper closure after the
event is nearer to the object's old position than to its new one, and even successful episodes at first move more toward the old position, while failures move decisively toward neither (Table~\ref{tab:diag}). The first closure thus does not separate the groups; recovery depends on whether a few regrasps succeed before time runs out, and more computation at the next scheduled query, where the new position is already visible, does not help (Section~\ref{sec:event}).
This suggests that a new query may not re-target the moved object and that
the actions themselves may need to be shifted; Section~\ref{sec:results} tests both.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Flow-based policies sample the initial noise of the integration, and this
noise alone moves the success of $(2,10)$ on the disturbed episodes between
63.2\% and 67.8\% across seven seeds (``noise draws''). We compare every variant with the \emph{per-episode mean of the seven
noise draws} of the base policy on the same 500 (task, initial state) episodes with the same disturbance, and report the mean
paired difference with a task-stratified bootstrap 95\% interval (6,000
resamples). Variants run once carry their single draw's noise and resolve only
differences of several points. Detector, regressor and teachers are each trained once and the students with three distillation seeds (the released one unless stated), all on initial states 0--24 only; initial states 25--49 of the same ten tasks (250 episodes, base 62.2\%) are held out from training for the comparisons in Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}; results on all 500 episodes include the training initial states. States 25--49 also served to choose gates, $\alpha$ and model versions, so they are held out from training but not from model selection. We therefore tested the released pipeline once, under a pre-registered protocol, on 500 fresh episodes: states 50--99 of each task's full initial-state file, unused by any earlier rollout of ours (the benchmark uses 0--49; five base draws, two or three per method). No new task is tested, and intervals are per comparison, without multiplicity correction.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Detecting the Displacement and Replanning at Once}
Running beside the controller, the detector receives at every control step, for both cameras (third-person and wrist), the previous and current frames and their difference at $64\times64$ grayscale, plus the action executed between them and the measured end-effector motion; a four-layer CNN and a small MLP fuse these into one
logit (0.1\,M parameters). Positives are the frame pairs straddling a displacement, hard negatives the pairs within 15
steps of it, plus 130 undisturbed training-state episodes; training uses the 500 events of two disturbed training-state runs, and the threshold is set for 0.05 false alarms per episode on 120 further undisturbed training-state episodes.

When the logit crosses the threshold, the schedule interrupts the current
chunk and queries the policy immediately, so within a few control steps of
the event the robot executes a chunk planned for the new scene
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a)). Policy-query time stays at $1.00\times$ the base schedule's (37.2 versus 37.4 queries per episode): each detection (1.07 per disturbed episode) adds at most one query (1.7\% more queries per step), but episodes end earlier on average;
a variant that also spends $n=10$ steps on the three queries after the event
takes $1.02\times$.

\subsection{Residual Correction}
We add a residual $\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^3$ to the translational part of every
action of the chunk returned by a query,
\begin{equation}
a_j^{\mathrm{xyz}} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}(a_j^{\mathrm{xyz}}+\Delta,-1,1),\qquad
\Delta=\mathbf{s}\odot\tanh u(x),
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{s}=(0.3,0.3,0.15)$ in LIBERO action units (0.3 units move the
end effector by about 3.6\,mm per control step), computed once per query from a
feature vector $x$ by a two-layer MLP $u$ with 256 units. The residual is \emph{gated}: it is zero except for a bounded window after
a detected displacement, so undisturbed behavior is touched only on false
alarms.

\noindent\textbf{Scripted shift (privileged).}
As a reference, once the simulator reports at a scheduled query that an object of interest has moved by $d$ since the reset (more than 2\,cm, not lifted), we add a nominal $\alpha d$ of end-effector motion, split over the $kh$ actions from this query on, horizontal only and without interrupting the chunk. With $k=2$, $\alpha=1$ performs like $0.5$ (71.2\% and 72.2\% on all 500, one draw) and $\alpha=1.5$ worse (64.8\%), presumably by adding to the base policy's own partial correction. A vision-only counterpart, the \emph{fixed-rule shift}, replans at each detection and adds $0.5\,\hat d$ likewise,
with $\hat d$ from the regressor below.

\noindent\textbf{Privileged teacher.}
We then learn the correction with residual RL~\cite{johannink2019residual} on privileged state. Choosing the residual at each gated query is a sequential decision problem, as in our earlier RL work~\cite{ji2026hrl,ji2024sparse,ji2026agentic}. The teacher's 53 inputs are proprioception, a summary of the base chunk, the relative position, displacement and presence of up to three objects of interest, episode progress, gripper state, queries since the gate opened, the previous residual and a task one-hot. Its gate, checked at every scheduled query (no replan at the event),
opens when an object has jumped more than 2.5\,cm since the previous query
while it rests within 1.5\,cm of its initial height and the open gripper is over 5\,cm away, and closes once the object is lifted 3\,cm. It
opens in 85\% of disturbed and in none of 250 undisturbed held-out episodes;
a cumulative 2-cm gate opened in 54\% of the undisturbed ones and, with a learned teacher (trained, like ours, only on disturbed episodes), cut their
success to 73.6\%. At gated query $i$ the actor samples $z_i\sim\mathcal{N}(u_\theta(x_i),\operatorname{diag}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2)$ ($\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ initially 0.30) and applies $\Delta_i=\mathbf{s}\odot\tanh z_i$. We first clone the scripted residual $\Delta^{\mathrm{sh}}_i$ ($\alpha=0.5$, $k=2$, triggered by the teacher's gate) from 441 episodes by minimizing \eqref{eq:bc} ($\oslash$: element-wise division), then run PPO with generalized advantage estimation~\cite{schulman2016gae} (settings in the code release) for 40 iterations of 120 episodes on the training initial states, maximizing \eqref{eq:ppo} with success $R\in\{0,1\}$ at the last of $T$ gated queries, $\gamma=0.99$ and a fixed rather than adaptive weight $c=0.01$~\cite{ji2026moreward,hwang2026arw}; one run takes about two GPU-hours.

\noindent\textbf{Vision-only student.}
The student may use neither object positions nor the privileged
gate. Its gate is the learned detector, active for six queries after each
detection; a firing inside the window restarts it, and every firing, true or
false, costs one replan. When the detector fires, a \emph{displacement
regressor} estimates the horizontal displacement $\hat d$ from the
$128\times128$ RGB frame pairs of both cameras, the executed action and the end-effector motion at the firing step (four ResNet blocks, 0.5\,M parameters, trained on 1,499 training-state events of
2--10\,cm; on 1,499 events from held-out initial states, median error 1.6\,cm at a median
displacement of 6.1\,cm, median direction error $9^\circ$). The student MLP $u_\phi$ sees the teacher's inputs with the object state replaced by $\hat d$, its norm and an active flag (36 inputs $\tilde x$, fusing $\hat d$ with the base chunk~\cite{kang2026fusion}). It is trained with DAgger~\cite{ross2011dagger} to match the teacher's greedy residual $\Delta^\star_i=\mathbf{s}\odot\tanh u_\theta(x_i)$, zero outside the teacher's gate, in three rounds of two rollouts per training state (10\% undisturbed) under the student's gate, its residual replaced at each gated query by the teacher's with probability $\beta=1,0.5,0$. Round $r$ minimizes \eqref{eq:distill} over the student-gated queries $\mathcal{D}_{\le r}$ of rounds up to $r$ (8,148 labels after three). The three objectives are
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}(\theta)&=\textstyle\sum_i\big\|u_\theta(x_i)-\operatorname{artanh}(\Delta^{\mathrm{sh}}_i\oslash\mathbf{s})\big\|^2,\label{eq:bc}\\
J(\theta)&=\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma^{T-1}R-c\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{i}\|\Delta_i\oslash\mathbf{s}\|^2\big],\label{eq:ppo}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DAgger}}(\phi)&=\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{\le r}}\big\|\mathbf{s}\odot\tanh u_\phi(\tilde x_i)-\Delta^\star_i\big\|^2.\label{eq:distill}
\end{align}

\subsection{Inference Levers Recover Little}\label{sec:levers}
Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} (top, held out) shows fixed settings of the levers that adaptive-inference methods tune, not those methods themselves. Replanning twice as often, $(2,5)$, trends down
($-4.2$) and five times as often, $(2,2)$, loses ten points (87.9\% undisturbed), and every schedule tested falls 29--33 points below its own undisturbed success (all 500). Five times more integration steps, $(10,10)$, change nothing measurable ($+1.0$). Selecting one of $K=8$ sampled chunks for the seven queries after the (oracle-timed) event also recovers little: random $-0.6$, medoid $+0.6$, a privileged rule aiming at the displaced object $+3.0$. 


\subsection{When to Replan: At the Event}\label{sec:event}
The base policy already replans at the next scheduled query. On held-out episodes, spending $n=10$ steps on that query and up to two more (oracle event timing) gains nothing measurable ($+0.6$ [$-2.6$, $+4.1$]), whereas, with the same oracle, moving the query \emph{to} the displacement gains $+3.7$ at $(2,10)$ and $+4.5$ with $n=10$ on the three queries from it, the latter $+3.9$ [$+0.4$, $+7.3$] above the next-query variant. The learned detector realizes most of this without the
oracle ($+2.8$ and $+4.2$; all 500: Table~\ref{tab:rec}) at $1.00\times$ and $1.02\times$ the policy-query time. In closed loop it detects 87--90\% of the displacements on held-out initial states within three control steps (98--100\% on training states), with 0.10--0.15 false alarms per disturbed episode, and leaves undisturbed success at
95.2\% on all 500 (base 95.4\%). On the fresh initial states its replan gains only $+0.2$ [$-2.4$, $+2.8$] (two draws; inconclusive), so the small gain above is not confirmed.

\subsection{How Much to Correct: Residual Recovery}\label{sec:correct}
 On held-out episodes the privileged scripted shift and the fixed-rule shift by $0.5\,\hat d$, which adds a replan at each detection, gain about the same ($+6.1$ and $+5.7$, three draws each; Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}). With privileged $d$, the detector's replan plus the scripted shift reaches 71.3\% held out (three draws), $+6.4$ [$+2.9$, $+10.1$] above the replan alone: shifting the actions adds to replanning. Learning the correction
helps further: the privileged teacher, whose correction is longer and more gradual than the scripted shift's, reaches 75.1\% on held-out
episodes ($+12.9$ [$+9.5$, $+16.6$], five draws), $+4.3$ [$+0.5$, $+8.4$]
above its behavior-cloned start (70.8\%, one draw); PPO from scratch reaches 72.2\% (two draws), 2.9 points [$-0.4$, $+6.3$] below the teacher. We did not distill a student from the behavior-cloned start, so PPO's share of the student's gain is not measured.

The distilled student comes within a few points of its teacher
without any privileged input: 71.8\% held out ($+9.6$ [$+5.8$, $+13.4$], five draws;
$-4.4$ [$-7.3$, $-1.6$] against the teacher with the same replan) and
75.9\% on all 500 episodes (Table~\ref{tab:rec}). Held out, it is $+3.9$ [$+0.9$, $+6.9$] above the fixed-rule shift and $+3.9$ [$+1.2$, $+6.8$] above the best of seven rules on training states ($\alpha=0.5$, $k=4$; all 500: $+4.7$, $+4.8$). On undisturbed episodes the
student (active in 5\% through false alarms) scores 95.4\%, as does the base draw with the same noise (one episode flips each way); against three base draws it is $+0.3$ [$-1.0$, $+1.7$], so the 95\% interval excludes drops above about one point. In the pre-registered test on fresh initial states the student gains $+8.4$ [$+5.6$, $+11.2$] (73.7\% versus 65.4\%) and $+2.9$ [$+0.7$, $+5.1$] over the fixed-rule shift, which itself gains $+5.4$; undisturbed success changes by $-0.2$ [$-1.0$, $+0.4$] (one draw). Against the 32.6\,ms GPU policy query, the detector takes 0.4\,ms per control step, the regressor 2.2\,ms per detection and the student MLP 0.02\,ms per active query (two CPU threads; full loop not timed).

\subsection{Other Disturbances and Side Effects}
The student and detector were trained only on 4--6\,cm displacements, mostly during the approach (the regressor on 2--10\,cm); Fig.~\ref{fig:general} evaluates them unchanged on other disturbances (all 500 episodes). With 8--12\,cm displacements, where the base policy falls to 37.0\% (three draws), the student gains $+8.3$ [$+5.0$, $+11.5$] and the privileged teacher $+12.1$ (two draws each), while the fixed-rule shift gains only $+3.8$ (three draws). The student is $+4.5$ [$+1.5$, $+7.5$] above it ($+4.2$ above the selected rule, two draws). Beyond the regressor's 10\,cm training range the student still gains $+8.0$ [$+3.7$, $+12.4$] ($+7.7$ [$+1.9$, $+13.6$] on held-out initial states). On the other disturbances the student is neutral: for a late move of the nearest free object
(steps 100--200) the detector fires in half of the episodes and the student's gain is within noise ($+1.4$), whereas the privileged teacher is slightly harmful ($-2.4$ [$-4.2$, $-0.5$], two draws each); for sliding (6--10\,cm planned, cut short when the gripper closes, so slides differ by method) and a five-step arm push the detector, trained on teleports, rarely fires (6--12\%) and success
changes by at most 1.1 points. Undisturbed success is 95.2--95.6\% in every detector-gated variant measured: the detector gate bounds how often a residual can act, whereas the scripted shift's cumulative gate costs 4.6 points (90.8\%).

\subsection{Ablation: Displacement Estimate}
A student distilled without $\hat d$ is only slightly worse than the full student on all 500 episodes ($-1.4$ [$-3.5$, $+0.9$], two draws; $-1.4$ to $-2.8$ over three distillation seeds): at 4--6\,cm the detector tells it \emph{when}, while the base chunk, proprioception and the task input may tell it \emph{how}. At 8--12\,cm $\hat d$ helps in two of three seeds (without it $-3.9$ [$-6.5$, $-1.3$], two draws; other seeds $-6.0$, $+0.8$). It does not make the correction proportional: held out, from 4--6 to 8--12\,cm the student's shift along the displacement grows only from 2.3 to 3.2\,cm (the teacher's from 3.3 to 9.2\,cm), as $\hat d$ saturates near 6\,cm.

\subsection{Where the Residual Helps and Where It Fails}
Gains concentrate where the approach triggers the event: held out, the student gains $+12.3$ [$+7.6$, $+17.1$] on the 179 approach-triggered episodes but $+2.7$ [$-2.6$, $+7.9$] on the 71 triggered by the time fallback (fresh states: $+11.3$ and $-0.1$). The residual was active in 85\% of the student's remaining held-out failures, so few are due to missed detections; the correction itself, labeled zero where the teacher's gate stayed closed, is a likely bottleneck. In Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}, although $\hat d$ is poor (1\,cm, wrong direction), the correction points toward the mug, so it may come from the base chunk or a task-level push.


% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

When the target object is displaced, $\pi_{0.5}$ often first closes the gripper
nearer its old position and loses 31 points. Replanning at the detected event recovers zero to three points, a
fixed-rule shift about six, and, on fresh initial states, the event-gated residual student about eight. Gating keeps this post-training conservative: undisturbed success shows no measurable change, whereas a loosely gated teacher cut it to 73.6\%. Limitations are one policy, benchmark and task set, and mostly single training runs. Needing no object state and little CPU time, the modules could run beside a robot's VLA, like TRANSIC's residual beside a simulation-trained policy~\cite{jiang2024transic}. Open sim-to-real gaps are rendered training frames, teleported rather than physical displacements, and synchronous execution; next, we probe them on hardware, test whether the correction tracks the displacement and try an end-to-end, possibly multi-agent gate~\cite{shin2024mars}.
