\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action policies execute predicted action chunks open loop, so when an object is suddenly displaced, the robot keeps following a plan made for the old scene. On LIBERO-10, displacing the target object by 4 to 6 cm, mostly as the gripper approaches, cuts the pi0.5 policy's success from 95 to 65 percent. Under one protocol, simple inference-time levers (replan frequency, integration steps, chunk selection) recover little, and replanning helps only when timed at the event: a learned detector on camera frames recognizes 87 to 90 percent of held-out displacements within three control steps and triggers a replan that gains about three points at unchanged inference cost. A fixed-rule shift toward the estimated displacement after this replan raises the total gain to about six points; a learned residual, a lightweight post-training stage with no gradients through the policy, gains the most. A privileged teacher, trained by behavior cloning of a scripted shift, then proximal policy optimization, is distilled with DAgger into an event-gated student that uses only proprioception, the predicted chunk and, through a displacement regressor, the camera frames. On all 500 disturbed episodes, half from training initial states, the student raises success from 64.7 to 75.9 percent (11.2 points, 95 percent interval 8.5 to 13.9; 9.6 points on the held-out half), gains 8.3 points on displacements twice as large, and leaves undisturbed success unchanged at 95.4 percent. Three distillation seeds give 75.9 to 76.8 percent on all 500; the displacement estimate helps modestly, and a behavior-cloned start is not required.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have become a standard recipe for
language-conditioned manipulation~\cite{kim2024openvla,black2024pi0,pi05}.
Most now predict \emph{action chunks}~\cite{zhao2023act,chi2025diffusion}:
one query of the model yields a short sequence of $H$ actions, of which the robot
executes a prefix of $h$ steps before the model is queried again. Chunking smooths motion and amortizes the backbone's cost but makes the
controller open loop between queries. If the scene changes
inside a chunk, the robot keeps executing actions planned for the old scene
until the chunk is spent, and the next query sees a configuration that the training data
rarely contains.

\input{fig_overview_res}

How large is this cost, and what recovers it? We study the question with the
official $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint for LIBERO~\cite{pi05,liu2023libero} under
a controlled disturbance: when the open gripper first comes within 12\,cm of
the object it is approaching (or at a random step if it has not by then), the object is teleported 4--6\,cm horizontally,
which cuts success on LIBERO-10 from 95.4\% to 64.7\%. Tracking the object
shows why (Section~\ref{sec:diag}): in about six of ten episodes the policy first closes the gripper nearer the \emph{old} position than the new one, and either recovers within a few attempts or loops until the
episode times out.

Recent work offers several responses. Adaptive-inference methods decide
when to replan, how much to compute per query, or which of several sampled
chunks to keep~\cite{liang2026aac,pan2026corrector,chun2025dasip,liu2025bid}; runtime
monitors detect execution failures~\cite{agia2024sentinel,xu2025faildetect,gu2025safe};
and residual reinforcement learning (RL) learns corrections on top of a fixed policy~\cite{yuan2025decorator,xiao2025pld}.
They are evaluated on different tasks and disturbances, so it is unclear how
much each kind of mechanism recovers when a static scene changes abruptly; we test each kind under one protocol. The inference levers recover little (Section~\ref{sec:levers}). Replanning \emph{at} the displacement helps where waiting for the next
scheduled query does not, and a learned detector realizes most of this gain at unchanged inference cost; but the new chunk corrects only partly toward the new position,
so the actions themselves must be corrected. A fixed-rule shift toward the
estimated displacement recovers about six points. A residual policy trained with RL as lightweight post-training around the policy, gated by the
detector and distilled into a student without privileged inputs, recovers 11 of the 31 lost points on all 500 episodes
(9.6 on the held-out half; Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a controlled mid-execution displacement protocol for LIBERO-10,
        complementing studies that perturb the setup before execution~\cite{fei2025liberoplus}
        or the robot's actions during it~\cite{guo2026robustvla,gao2026ogpo}, a diagnosis of how a chunked VLA
        fails, and an evaluation protocol that compares every variant with
        the mean of seven sampling-noise draws of the base policy on
        identical episodes;
  \item a side-by-side comparison showing that simple inference levers do not
        recover the loss and that plain replanning helps only when timed at the event and even then recovers only about three of 31 points, with a learned per-step detector (AUROC 0.999)
        that triggers it at $1.00\times$ the cost;
  \item an event-gated residual recovery policy, trained as a privileged teacher by behavior cloning (BC) of a scripted
        shift followed by proximal policy optimization (PPO) and distilled into
        a vision-only student, that, on all 500 episodes, adds 4.7 points over a fixed-rule shift,
        transfers to displacements twice as large and does not harm
        undisturbed episodes.\footnote{Code, trained modules and per-episode results:\newline \url{https://github.com/BitAdventurer/VLA-Disturbance-Recovery}}
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Adaptive chunk execution.}
AAC sets the executed prefix of a chunk from the entropy of sampled
chunks~\cite{liang2026aac}, bidirectional decoding selects among chunks
resampled at every step~\cite{liu2025bid}, RTC inpaints the next chunk
while the current one executes~\cite{black2025rtc}, DVAC executes the
low-variance prefix of a chunk and replans before its high-variance
actions~\cite{feng2026dvac}, and DA-SIP chooses the number of integration
steps per query with a difficulty classifier~\cite{chun2025dasip}. They balance reactivity, consistency and compute but detect no events. VLA-Corrector~\cite{pan2026corrector} does: it truncates the chunk when visual latents drift from a learned prediction and replans with gradient guidance, but adds no correction policy.

\noindent\textbf{Runtime monitoring.}
Sentinel checks temporal action consistency and, with a vision-language
model, task progress~\cite{agia2024sentinel}, FAIL-Detect applies conformal
sequential out-of-distribution detection~\cite{xu2025faildetect}, and SAFE
learns a VLA failure score from internal features~\cite{gu2025safe}; these
monitors flag failures or halt the policy. Like VLA-Corrector, we use detection to
\emph{act}, but on one specific external event, and we also gate a learned
correction.

\noindent\textbf{Correcting a fixed policy's actions.}
Residual RL~\cite{johannink2019residual} refines imitation-learned policies~\cite{yuan2025decorator,ankile2024resip} and a
frozen $\pi_0$, with the corrections then distilled into the VLA~\cite{xiao2025pld}, and latent-noise RL steers $\pi_0$ without changing its weights~\cite{wagenmaker2025dsrl}. Other work post-trains the VLA itself with online
RL~\cite{chen2025conrft,guo2025irevla}, which has been shown to help when an
object is moved mid-episode~\cite{liu2025rl4vla}, or with offline RL to recover from errors~\cite{gao2026ogpo}. A training-free operator offsets and
compresses a chunk toward perceived motion~\cite{zhang2026pacepath}, the
closest analogue of our fixed-rule shift. None of these corrections is
gated by an event; ours is zero except after a detected displacement,
distilled from a privileged teacher into a vision-only student as
in~\cite{ankile2024resip}, and compared with a fixed-rule shift on the same
episodes.

\section{Setting and Diagnosis}\label{sec:diag}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Chunked Execution and the Disturbance}
A flow-based VLA maps an observation $o_t$ and instruction $\ell$ to a
chunk $\mathbf{A}_t=(a_t,\dots,a_{t+H-1})$ by integrating a learned velocity
field for $n$ Euler steps~\cite{black2024pi0,lipman2022flow}; the robot
executes $h\le H$ actions and queries again. The same family of conditional
generative models served our brain-network generation~\cite{oh2026ptdiff}. We use the released $\pi_{0.5}$ LIBERO checkpoint of openpi~\cite{pi05} with
$H=10$ and, unless stated otherwise, $(n,h)=(2,10)$ (95.4\% undisturbed); openpi's example setting $(10,5)$ reaches 91.9\% (three runs) at $2.8\times$ the cost and loses as much as $(2,10)$ under the displacement defined below (to 61.2\%, two draws; $-30.7$, 95\% interval [$-34.4$, $-27.0$]).
Episodes follow the released script (520
control steps at 20\,Hz after 10 settling steps) on the 50 fixed initial
states of each of the ten tasks, 500 episodes per run.

The \emph{object displacement} is applied once per episode: when the open gripper first comes within 12\,cm (end effector to object center)
of a movable object of interest (an object named in the task goal) that it
does not hold, that object is teleported horizontally by a distance drawn from
$[4,6]$\,cm in a uniformly random direction and its velocity is zeroed. If no approach has occurred by a random step in 50--170 (about 26\% of episodes, mostly in two tasks), the nearest free object of interest is displaced at that step. Direction and magnitude are seeded by the task and initial state, so every method sees identical displacement vectors. Success drops to 64.7\% (mean of seven noise draws), and every
failure is a timeout.

\subsection{Why the Policy Fails}
We tracked the displaced object in 100 disturbed episodes (initial states 0--9 of each task, one base draw; Table~\ref{tab:diag}). All successes eventually lift it, but 46\% of
failures never do. In about 62\% of \emph{both} groups the first gripper closure after the
event is nearer to the object's old position than to its new one, and even successful episodes at first move more toward the old position (cosine 0.69
versus 0.60); failures move decisively toward neither (0.26 / 0.39). The
policy does not fail because it cannot see the object: the new position is in
the image at the next query, yet replanning at the next query does not help (Section~\ref{sec:event}),
and recovery depends on whether a few regrasps succeed before time runs out.
This suggests that replanning helps only if it happens \emph{at} the event, and
that the actions themselves must be shifted.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Flow-based policies sample the initial noise of the integration, and this
noise alone moves the success of $(2,10)$ on the disturbed episodes between
63.2\% and 67.8\% across seven seeds (``noise draws''), so single draws are
noisy. We compare every variant with the \emph{per-episode mean of the seven
noise draws} of the base policy on the same 500 (task, initial state) episodes with the same disturbance, and report the mean
paired difference with a task-stratified bootstrap 95\% interval (6,000
resamples). Variants run once carry that draw's noise and resolve only
differences of several points. Detector, regressor and teachers are each trained once and the students with three distillation seeds (the released one unless stated), all on initial states 0--24 only; initial states 25--49 (250 episodes, base 62.2\%) are held out from training for the main comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}); results on all 500 episodes include the training initial states.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Detecting the Displacement and Replanning at Once}
The detector runs beside the controller at every control step and
receives, for each of the two cameras (third-person and wrist), the
previous frame, the current frame and their difference at $64\times64$
grayscale, together with the action executed between them and the measured
end-effector motion; a four-layer CNN and a small MLP fuse these into one
logit (0.1\,M parameters). Hand-crafted signals (camera, backbone-embedding and chunk changes) reach query-level AUROCs of only 0.50--0.83 on training-state episodes, against 0.999 (per step, held out) for this network. Positives are the frame pairs straddling a displacement, hard negatives the pairs within 15
steps of it, plus 250 undisturbed training-state episodes; training on the 500 events of
two disturbed runs that fall on the training initial states takes seven
minutes, and the threshold is set on that split for 0.05 false alarms per undisturbed episode.

When the logit crosses the threshold, the schedule interrupts the current
chunk and queries the policy immediately, so within a few control steps of
the event the robot executes a chunk planned for the new scene
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a)). Each detection adds one query, yet the measured inference cost per episode stays at $1.00\times$ the base schedule;
a variant that also spends $n=10$ steps on the three queries after the event
costs $1.02\times$.

\subsection{Residual Correction}
We add a residual $\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^3$ to the translational part of every
action of the chunk returned by a query,
\begin{equation}
a_j^{\mathrm{xyz}} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}(a_j^{\mathrm{xyz}}+\Delta,-1,1),\qquad
\Delta=\mathbf{s}\odot\tanh u(x),
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{s}=(0.3,0.3,0.15)$ in LIBERO action units (0.3 units move the
end effector by about 3.6\,mm per control step), computed once per query from a
feature vector $x$ by a two-layer MLP $u$ with 256 units. The residual is \emph{gated}: it is zero except for a bounded window after
a detected displacement, so undisturbed behavior is touched only on false
alarms.

\noindent\textbf{Scripted shift (privileged).}
As a probe, once the simulator reports at a scheduled query that an object of interest has moved by $d$ since the reset (more than 2\,cm, not lifted), we add $\alpha d$ divided equally over the $kH$
actions of the next $k$ queries ($0.5\,d$ is about 0.1 units per action),
horizontal only and without interrupting the chunk. With $k=2$, $\alpha=0.5$ and $\alpha=1$ perform alike (72.2\%
and 71.2\% on all 500 episodes in one draw; Table~\ref{tab:rec} averages three) while $\alpha=1.5$ falls back to 64.8\%:
overcorrecting presumably adds to the base policy's own partial correction once it sees
the new scene, so the residual should complement it. A vision-only counterpart, the \emph{fixed-rule shift}, replans at each detection and adds $0.5\,\hat d$ in the same way,
with $\hat d$ from the displacement regressor below.

\noindent\textbf{Privileged teacher.}
We then learn the correction with residual RL~\cite{johannink2019residual} on privileged state. Deciding how much to intervene at every gated query is a sequential
decision problem of the kind we have solved with RL for brain-connectivity
segmentation~\cite{ji2026hrl}, graph sparsification~\cite{ji2024sparse} and
agentic diagnosis~\cite{ji2026agentic}. The teacher's 53 inputs are proprioception, a summary of the base chunk, the relative position, displacement and presence of up to three objects of interest, episode progress, gripper state, queries since the gate opened, the previous residual and a task one-hot. Its gate, checked at every scheduled query (no replan at the event),
opens when an object has jumped more than 2.5\,cm since the previous query
while resting at its initial height (within 1.5\,cm), with the gripper open
and more than 5\,cm away, and closes once the object is lifted 3\,cm; it
opens in 85\% of disturbed and 0 of 250 undisturbed held-out episodes,
whereas a cumulative 2-cm gate opened in 54\% of the undisturbed ones and, with a learned teacher behind it, cut undisturbed
success to 73.6\%. The actor is Gaussian over $u$ (initial standard deviation 0.30). We first clone the
scripted shift ($\alpha=0.5$, $k=2$) from about 450 episodes, then run PPO with generalized advantage
estimation~\cite{schulman2016gae} ($\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$, clip 0.2,
eight epochs, target KL 0.02, actor learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, one
critic-only warm-up iteration) for 40 iterations of 120 episodes on the
training initial states. The reward is the episode success minus $0.01\,\|\Delta/\mathbf{s}\|^2$ per gated query, a penalty whose
weight we fixed rather than adapted as in our earlier RL
pipelines~\cite{ji2026moreward,hwang2026arw}; one run takes about two GPU-hours.

\noindent\textbf{Vision-only student.}
The deployed residual may use neither object positions nor the privileged
gate. Its gate is the learned detector, active for six queries after each
detection; a firing inside the window restarts it, and every firing, true or
false, costs one replan. When the detector fires, a \emph{displacement
regressor} estimates the horizontal displacement $\hat d$ from the
$128\times128$ RGB frame pair of both cameras at the firing step (four ResNet blocks, 0.5\,M parameters; 1,499 training-state events of
2--10\,cm, 1,499 held-out events: median error 1.6\,cm at a median
displacement of 6.1\,cm, median direction error $9^\circ$). The student MLP sees proprioception, the base-chunk summary,
$\hat d$ and its norm, an active flag, the queries since the gate opened,
episode progress, the gripper state, the previous residual and the task
one-hot (36 inputs), so it can weigh the explicit estimate against the
implicit drift of the base chunk, in the spirit of our adaptive fusion of
static and dynamic connectivity features~\cite{kang2026fusion}. It is
distilled from the teacher's mean action with DAgger~\cite{ross2011dagger}:
three rounds of two rollouts per training initial state under the student's gate
and replan, the teacher's residual replacing the student's at each gated
query with probability $\beta=1,0.5,0$. Every gated query is labeled with
the teacher's residual (zero where its privileged gate is closed; 8,148
labels) and the student is refit by least squares on $\Delta$.

\subsection{Inference Levers Recover Little}\label{sec:levers}
Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} (top, held out) shows simple versions of the levers that adaptive-inference methods tune. Replanning twice as often, $(2,5)$, trends down
($-4.2$ [$-9.0$, $+0.7$]) and five times as often, $(2,2)$, loses ten points ($-10.2$ [$-15.7$, $-4.6$]) and is also worse undisturbed (87.9\%). With five times more integration steps, $(10,10)$ is indistinguishable from the base policy ($+1.0$ [$-3.7$, $+5.8$]). Selecting one of $K=8$ sampled chunks for the seven queries after the (oracle-timed) event also recovers little: random $-0.6$, medoid $+0.6$, a privileged rule aiming at the displaced object $+3.0$ [$-1.1$, $+7.1$]. 


\subsection{When to Replan: At the Event}\label{sec:event}
On held-out episodes, replanning only from the \emph{next scheduled} query gains nothing measurable even with $n=10$ and oracle event timing ($+0.6$ [$-2.6$, $+4.1$]). Replanning \emph{at} the displacement with the same oracle gains $+3.7$ [$+0.7$, $+6.7$] at $(2,10)$ and $+4.5$ [$+1.2$, $+7.7$] with $n=10$ for the following queries, the latter $+3.9$ [$+0.4$, $+7.3$] above the next-query replan. The learned detector realizes most of this gain without the
oracle: $+2.8$ [$+0.1$, $+5.4$] and $+4.2$ [$+1.2$, $+7.2$] on held-out
episodes (all 500: Table~\ref{tab:rec}), at $1.00\times$ and $1.02\times$ the inference cost. In closed loop it detects 87--90\% of the held-out displacements within three control steps (98--100\% on the training initial states), with 0.10--0.15 false alarms per disturbed episode, and leaves undisturbed success at
95.2\% on all 500 (base 95.4\%).

\subsection{How Much to Correct: Residual Recovery}
 On held-out episodes the privileged scripted shift and the fixed-rule shift by $0.5\,\hat d$, which adds a replan at each detection, gain about the same ($+6.1$ and $+5.7$, three draws each; Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}). With privileged $d$, the detector's replan plus the scripted shift reaches 71.3\% held out (three draws), $+6.4$ [$+2.9$, $+10.1$] above the replan alone and $+3.1$ [$-0.1$, $+6.4$] above the shift alone. Learning the correction
helps further: the privileged teacher reaches 75.1\% on held-out
episodes ($+12.9$ [$+9.5$, $+16.6$], five draws), $+6.9$ [$+3.5$, $+10.2$]
above the scripted shift on the same episodes, with a longer and more gradual correction. With the detector's replan it reaches 76.1\% ($+1.0$ [$-1.7$, $+4.0$] over the teacher alone, three draws), so on top of a learned correction the replan's share is not resolved.

The distilled student comes within a few points of its teacher
without any privileged input: 71.8\% held out ($+9.6$ [$+5.8$, $+13.4$];
$-4.4$ [$-7.3$, $-1.6$] against the teacher run with the same replan) and
75.9\% on all 500 episodes ($+11.2$ [$+8.5$, $+13.9$], five draws; mean 76.4\% (75.9--76.8\%) over three distillation seeds), $+4.7$
[$+2.3$, $+7.0$] above the fixed-rule shift and $+4.8$ [$+2.7$, $+7.0$] above the rule selected on training initial states among seven settings ($\alpha=0.5$ over $k=4$ queries: 71.1\%, three draws). On undisturbed episodes the
student (active in 5\% through false alarms) scores 95.4\%, as does the base policy; the pipeline adds a sub-millisecond CNN per step, one
regressor pass per firing and one MLP per query to a 33\,ms policy query (one RTX 5090).

\subsection{Generalization and Side Effects}
The student and detector were trained only on 4--6\,cm displacements, mostly during the approach (the regressor on 2--10\,cm); Fig.~\ref{fig:general} evaluates them unchanged on other disturbances (all 500 episodes). With 8--12\,cm displacements, where the base policy falls to 37.0\% (three draws), the student gains $+8.3$ [$+5.0$, $+11.5$] and the privileged teacher $+12.1$ (two draws each; the student $+5.8$ to $+8.8$ over three distillation seeds), while the fixed-rule shift gains only $+3.8$ [$+1.1$, $+6.5$] (three draws). The student is $+4.5$ [$+1.5$, $+7.5$] above it ($+4.2$ [$+1.3$, $+7.2$] above the selected rule, two draws), suggesting that the learned use of $\hat d$ transfers better. On the other disturbances the deployed system is neutral: for a displacement triggered late by time
(steps 100--200) the detector fires in half of the episodes and the student's gain is within noise ($+1.4$ [$-0.2$, $+3.0$]), whereas the privileged teacher is slightly harmful ($-2.4$ [$-4.2$, $-0.5$], two draws each); for sliding (6--10\,cm over two seconds) and a five-step arm push the detector, trained on teleports, rarely fires (6--12\%) and success
changes by at most 1.1 points. Undisturbed success is 95.2--95.6\% in every detector-gated variant measured: the detector gate bounds how often a residual can act, whereas the scripted shift's cumulative gate costs 4.6 points (90.8\%).

\subsection{Ablations}
\noindent\textbf{Displacement estimate.}
A student distilled without $\hat d$ is only slightly worse than the full student on all 500 episodes ($-1.4$ [$-3.5$, $+0.9$], two draws; $-1.4$ to $-2.8$ over three distillation seeds): at 4--6\,cm the detector tells it \emph{when} and the base chunk and proprioception likely tell it \emph{how}. At 8--12\,cm $\hat d$ helps in two of three seeds (without it: $-3.9$ [$-6.5$, $-1.3$] for the released student, two draws; $-6.0$ and $+0.8$ for the others, one draw each). It does not make the correction proportional: held out, the student's shift along the displacement grows from 2.3 to 3.2\,cm (the teacher's from 3.3 to 9.2\,cm), as the regressor saturates near 6\,cm.

\noindent\textbf{Scripted prior.}
PPO from scratch with the same gate and budget reaches 72.2\% on held-out
episodes (two draws, Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}):
RL alone finds the correction; PPO from the behavior-cloned start ends at 75.1\%, paired $+2.9$ [$-0.4$, $+6.3$] above PPO from scratch, within noise.

\subsection{Where the Residual Helps and Where It Fails}
Per task (all 500 episodes), the student's gains are largest in four tasks: bowl in
drawer $+13.1$, mug and pudding $+24.7$, soup and cheese $+18.8$ and two moka
pots $+19.3$ points. On the 63 episodes that
the base policy never solves in seven draws, the student succeeds in 36.5\% of
its runs, and on the 199 always solved, it keeps 95.1\%. The residual was active
in 91\% of the student's remaining failures (85\% held out), so missed detections explain few of them; the correction, limited to six queries and to translation, is the likely next
bottleneck.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

When the object it approaches is suddenly displaced, $\pi_{0.5}$ finishes the
stale chunk and loses 31 points. Simple inference-time levers do not recover this
loss; replanning at the detected event recovers about three points, a
fixed-rule shift about six, and an event-gated residual learned with RL ten (held out) to eleven (all 500). The gate keeps this cheap post-training conservative: a loosely gated teacher cut held-out undisturbed success from 95.2\% to 73.6\%, whereas the
gated residual leaves undisturbed success unchanged. Limits: one policy, benchmark and disturbance family (teleports) in simulation. Next: mixed disturbances, an end-to-end gate (possibly as cooperating agents~\cite{shin2024mars}), distillation into the VLA, and hardware.
